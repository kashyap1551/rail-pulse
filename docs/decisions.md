## Findings: Digitraffic history check (10 Nov 2026)
Checked the Swagger spec (rata.digitraffic.fi/swagger) and live calls.
- **Endpoint:** `GET /api/v1/trains/{departure_date}` (e.g. `/api/v1/trains/2026-11-09`) returns every train that departed that day, about 1,200–1,900 trains per day.
- **Fields per stop** (inside each train's `timeTableRows`): `scheduledTime`, `actualTime`, `differenceInMinutes` (minutes late), `cancelled`, `causes`, `stationShortCode`, `type` (ARRIVAL or DEPARTURE). Each train also has a top-level `cancelled` flag.
- **How far back:** spot checks returned data for 10 Nov of 2014, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025. 10 Nov 2013 and 10 Nov 2015 returned no trains, so there may be gaps. Not checked: the exact earliest date.
- **Implication:** enough history exists to define a "normal evening" (for example, the same weekday and hours over the past weeks or years) without storing anything myself. One day of data is a large download, so v2 will need to pick only the fields it needs.
- **Live positions** (`/api/v1/train-locations/latest`): checked 10 Nov 2026, returns about 155 trains and allows requests from any website (CORS `*`), so GitHub Pages can call it directly. Without gzip support the server answers 406; browsers send that automatically.
- **Click details (checked 10 Nov 2026):** `/api/v1/trains/{date}/{number}` gives train type, category, commuter line, and every stop with `commercialStop`, `liveEstimateTime` and `differenceInMinutes`. Rows without an actual time can appear in the middle of the route, so "next stop" is the first commercial stop after the last row that has an actual time.
- **Train types (checked 10 Nov 2026):** categories are Long-distance, Commuter, Cargo, Locomotive, Test drive, On-track machines and Shunting. Type codes seen include IC, IC2, S, P, PYO, H, HDM, HSM (long-distance), HL, HV, HLV (commuter), T, RJ (cargo). Digitraffic gives codes only, no descriptions, so calling a code "Pendolino" or "Regional" is my interpretation. Codes I could not place (for example PVS, PVV, MV, MUS, V) go in "Service & other".
- **Decided 12 Nov 2026: Digitraffic's track data (compared 11 Nov 2026).** The comparison below led to it; what was built, and how well it works, is in the next finding. Both sources had been downloaded and simplified the same way (Douglas-Peucker, 25 m tolerance, coordinates rounded to 5 decimals, about 1 m).
  - *Digitraffic* (`/infra-api/0.7/radat.geojson`): 173 official rail-line features, which become 3,259 line pieces and 8,825 km of line. The download is 12.6 MB in ETRS-TM35FIN metres, so it must be converted to latitude/longitude once. After simplifying: about 295 KB, or about 90 KB when the web server compresses it (10 m tolerance: 396 KB / 121 KB). Licence: Digitraffic says its open data is CC BY 4.0 with the example credit "Source: Fintraffic / digitraffic.fi, license CC 4.0 BY"; changes must be noted and the licence linked. Its page does not say whether the track dataset in particular is covered, so that is unconfirmed.
  - *OpenStreetMap* (Overpass query: `railway=rail` without a `service` tag, snapshot 11 Nov 2026): 8,538 ways, 6,441 km, 11 MB as downloaded. After simplifying: about 424 KB, or about 133 KB compressed (10 m: 486 KB / 154 KB). Licence: ODbL 1.0, credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors" with a link to openstreetmap.org/copyright (already shown on the map for the base map). Publishing an extract in the repo makes that file an ODbL "derivative database", which must stay under ODbL (share-alike) and say so; the page drawing it is only a "produced work" and needs just the credit. Not legal advice.
  - *Agreement:* every OSM main or branch line has a Digitraffic line within 150 m (100%), but only 90% of Digitraffic's line has an OSM line within 150 m. I did not investigate what the extra 10% is (possibly yards, closed lines, or lines overlapping each other, which would also explain the larger kilometre figure).
  - *How it was built:* a one-off Node script using only built-in modules (`tools/build-rail-network.js`, no packages) downloads the track lines, joins them into a network, keeps the lines real timetables use and writes `data/rail-network.json`. The page fetches it once, draws it under the trains and routes along it. The first research scripts (the comparison above) are kept in `tools/research/rail-lines/`.
- **Rail network and route accuracy (12 Nov 2026).**
  - *Network:* 3,257 line pieces (8,840 km, every track) → 9,015 lines between junctions after joining → 217 lines, 5,312 km kept after routing 8 days of timetables (11,459 trains, 547 different pairs of neighbouring timetable points, all 547 routed, still all 547 on the written file). Stations lie a median 1 m from the lines (90th percentile 5 m, 99th 31 m, furthest 380 m). To refresh the data: `node tools/build-rail-network.js` (downloads are cached in `tools/.cache`, which is not committed).
  - *Route check on three real trains* (yesterday, 11 Nov; every moving position above 5 km/h against the route the page builds for that train): commuter L 8402 Kirkkonummi → Helsinki, 37 km, 664 positions: median 5.0 m from the route line, 91% within 30 m, all within 100 m (the furthest 64 m; it runs on commuter tracks 4 to 20 m beside the line the route follows). InterCity 943 Helsinki → Turku, 192 km, 2,190 positions: median 0.4 m, 98.4% within 30 m, all within 100 m. Cargo T 2854 Kouvola → Riihimäki, 123 km, 2,028 positions: median 4.0 m, 98% within 30 m, 99.5% within 100 m; the furthest (276 m) is the last stretch inside Riihimäki's marshalling yard. All 75 legs of the three routes matched the lines (none dashed), and each route was 99% to 100% of the distance the train really travelled (37.0 vs 37.0 km, 192.2 vs 192.6, 123.4 vs 123.5).
  - *Wider check:* 15 more trains (4 commuter, 4 long-distance and 7 cargo, 11 Nov): all 248 legs matched, none dashed. Typical positions were within 0.3 to 5 m of the route (commuter trains in the Helsinki area up to a median 27 m, where several tracks run side by side). One cargo train (7158) had 35% of its positions far from its route: they are all in Riihimäki's marshalling yard, which is not in the network on purpose.
  - *Not verified:* the lines against the base map's own rails (the base map is OpenStreetMap, and both can be a few metres off the real track); a daytime run with many routes open; other days (a branch that no train used in the 8 days is missing); Safari and a real phone. Positions in yards and sidings are, by design, off the network.
